# Business Before Agents — v0.1 Preview

Business Before Agents is an industry-neutral, small-business-first framework for understanding a business before designing AI agents, automations, or new operating systems.

This preview is a **Business Discovery Pilot**. It can guide an adaptive interview, organize evidence, document assumptions, and produce a preliminary assessment. It does not autonomously operate a business, guarantee policy compliance, or replace legal, financial, security, or other professional advice.

## Why discovery comes before AI

There is no single, reliable failure rate that applies to every AI project. However, multiple studies point to the same practical pattern: organizations struggle when they begin with an AI tool before defining the business problem, workflow, available data, ownership, success measures, and human controls.

- A 2024 RAND study based on interviews with 65 experienced AI practitioners found that misunderstanding or miscommunicating the business problem was the most commonly reported failure pattern. Among the 50 industry participants, 84% cited one or more leadership-driven causes as a primary reason AI projects fail.
- BCG's 2024 study of more than 1,000 companies found that only 26% had developed the capabilities to move beyond proofs of concept and begin generating tangible AI value; 74% had not yet shown tangible value.
- McKinsey's 2026 global survey found that 37% of respondents reported a positive enterprise-level EBIT contribution from AI, while its “high performer” group remained about 6%. High performers were distinguished by workflow redesign, leadership commitment, and operating rigor—not simply access to better tools.
- A peer-reviewed study of 1,088 prospective founders found that formal business planning was associated with a 16.1-percentage-point difference in venture viability after matching comparable planners and nonplanners. This study concerns new-venture planning rather than AI, so it supports disciplined preparation—not a guarantee of success.

These findings are directional and come from different populations, definitions, and methods. They should not be combined into a single success or failure probability for an individual business. See [Evidence, limitations, and further reading](references/EVIDENCE_BASE.md) for source links, methodology notes, accessible news examples, and claims to avoid.

## Purpose and mindset

This process is not intended to judge, grade, audit, or criticize a business owner or the way the business has been operated. It creates an honest baseline that can identify opportunities for improvement while also recognizing strengths, progress, resilience, and successful practices already present in the business.

The quality of the result depends on candid reflection. Owners should not try to predict the next question or shape answers toward the recommendation they expect or want. An uncomfortable question, uncertainty, or weakness is not a failure; it may simply identify an area that deserves closer attention. At the same time, honesty does not require disclosing confidential or unnecessary information, and the owner may pause, decline, or defer any question.

At the beginning, the consultant asks for a simple acknowledgment that this purpose is understood. This is conversational—not a contract, legal consent, waiver, or promise to accept the recommendations.

## What to expect

This is intentionally more thorough than a quick AI-readiness quiz. AI recommendations are only as useful as the understanding beneath them, so the consultant first establishes how the business creates value, makes decisions, handles information, and responds when normal processes break down.

You do not need to complete the process in one sitting. You may stop at any time—even in the middle of a topic—and return later. When you pause, the consultant should create a **Resume Checkpoint** recording the approved understanding, remaining questions, and exact place to continue.

Keep the approved checkpoint with your private business-state record. If your AI project retains its files and conversation context, it can use that checkpoint when you return. If the platform does not retain the context, or you begin a new workspace, paste or upload the checkpoint to resume. The framework does not depend on the AI claiming to remember information it can no longer access.

Typical time ranges are:

- **Quick orientation — 20 to 30 minutes:** clarifies the business, desired outcome, immediate constraints, and the most important next area to investigate. This is useful for getting started but is not a complete baseline.
- **Practical business baseline — 90 minutes to 3 hours:** covers customers, offerings, operations, capabilities, economics, people, decisions, information, risks, and AI preferences. This is the recommended starting point and will often take two or more sessions.
- **Evidence-backed assessment — 3 to 8 hours or more:** adds document review, conflicting evidence, multiple participants, sensitive-data decisions, and validation of important claims. Established or complex businesses may require additional time.

These estimates cover discovery and assessment, not implementation. The actual time depends on the complexity of the business, the quality of its records, the number of people involved, and how deeply the owner wants to validate the results.

Questions may address:

- what the business provides, who it serves, and why customers choose it
- how work moves from initial demand through delivery and follow-up
- revenue, costs, capacity, bottlenecks, dependencies, and exceptions
- distinctive capabilities, relationships, knowledge, and intellectual property
- roles, recurring decisions, approvals, and accountability
- documents, systems, data quality, privacy, and conflicting information
- which activities should remain human-led and where AI assistance may be appropriate
- how success, failure, escalation, and rollback would be measured

Not every question will apply. The consultant should explain why a question matters, adapt to the owner's answers, and avoid collecting information that is unnecessary or unsuitable for the selected AI environment.

## How to use the recommendations

The resulting assessment is a suggested starting model for businesses beginning or improving their AI and agentic path. It is designed to support thoughtful planning and better outcomes, particularly for new AI users, but it cannot guarantee success or account for every condition affecting a business.

Each use case must be evaluated carefully for business fit, people, customers, finances, operations, data, privacy, security, legal obligations, and risk. Businesses should introduce changes cautiously, test them in appropriate stages, seek qualified advice where needed, and keep authorized people responsible for consequential decisions. Neither AI nor this assessment should be the sole decision factor.

This preview is a free, limited-scope service and is not a comprehensive professional engagement. When a proposed decision creates substantial financial exposure, or the owner has serious financial concerns, the assessment should be treated only as an initial baseline. The business should seek appropriately qualified financial, legal, operational, security, or business advice before proceeding.

The free baseline may be used as the starting point for a separate paid consultative engagement that reviews the business plan, evidence, risks, and recommendations more closely. Any paid engagement should disclose its own scope, provider qualifications, fees, deliverables, and terms. Completing the free assessment does not create an obligation to purchase another service.

Completed assessments include this disclosure and ask for a simple acknowledgment that it is understood. The acknowledgment is not an agreement to proceed, a waiver of any rights, or a commitment to purchase a paid service.

## Quick start

1. Choose a setup guide under `platforms/`.
2. Review `privacy/PRIVACY.md` before sharing business information.
3. Load the files in `core/` into your AI project's instructions or knowledge area.
4. Copy `templates/BUSINESS_STATE_TEMPLATE.md` into private, access-controlled storage.
5. Begin with: **“Start the Business Before Agents discovery interview.”**
6. Review and approve each checkpoint before the consultant treats it as established fact.
7. When you want to stop, say: **“Pause and create my resume checkpoint.”** Save the approved checkpoint with your private business-state file.
8. When you return, say: **“Resume from my last approved checkpoint.”** Provide the checkpoint if the AI project cannot retrieve it.

## Privacy boundary

The framework files may be public. Your business documents, interview answers, customer data, financial information, operating model, and completed templates should remain private. A hosted AI workspace is not the same as fully local processing; read the privacy guidance before use.

## Industry neutrality

The framework supports businesses in any lawful industry, including travel and hospitality. Project documentation, tutorials, walkthroughs, and demonstration examples deliberately avoid using travel as their primary business model so that the framework remains broadly representative.
