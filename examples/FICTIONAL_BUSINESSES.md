# Fictional Test Businesses

These examples test adaptability and are not preferred business models.

## Mobile repair service

A two-person company repairs bicycles and mobility equipment at customers' homes. Test scheduling, parts availability, field judgment, safety, and customer communication.

## Consumer-goods manufacturer

A small manufacturer produces refillable household-cleaning products for independent retailers. Test quality records, supplier constraints, batch planning, channel economics, and regulatory unknowns.

## Bookkeeping support firm

A remote team helps local organizations organize monthly records before licensed professionals complete tax or assurance work. Test authority boundaries, confidential data, service scope, exceptions, and review controls.

## Specialty construction contractor

A contractor restores older commercial interiors. Test project-based economics, estimating uncertainty, subcontractor coordination, safety, approvals, and tacit expertise.

## Editorial rule

Travel and hospitality businesses are supported by the framework, but project documentation, tutorials, walkthroughs, and demonstrations should not use travel as the primary example. This prevents the documentation from being shaped around the project creator's separate travel-planning work.
