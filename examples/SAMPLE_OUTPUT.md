# Sample Output — The Toy Scout

> **Fictional, abridged illustration.** This example shows the kind of practical guidance a business owner might receive after a quick orientation. It is not a completed assessment, does not reproduce the full discovery process or internal evaluation logic, and should not be used as a substitute for assessing a real business.

## Business snapshot

**The Toy Scout** is a fictional owner-operated business that finds vintage toys for online collectors and gift buyers. The owner monitors estate sales, online marketplaces, dealer groups, and local sellers; evaluates likely authenticity and condition; purchases selected items; and resells or sources them for clients.

The owner wants help finding promising inventory earlier, estimating reasonable buying and selling ranges, and producing consistent marketing without losing the judgment and personal trust that distinguish the business.

### Evidence boundary

This illustrative output assumes one owner interview and sample descriptions of current work. No sales history, marketplace agreements, customer records, financial statements, inventory data, or item photographs were reviewed. All conclusions are provisional.

## What appears to be working

- **Specialized judgment:** The owner recognizes model variations, incomplete sets, reproduction parts, and condition details that general price searches often miss.
- **Buyer trust:** Customers value honest descriptions and the owner's willingness to explain uncertainty.
- **Cross-market awareness:** The owner knows that asking prices are not the same as completed sales and that value changes by venue, timing, condition, and buyer type.
- **Curated service:** The business is not merely reselling products; it reduces search effort and purchasing risk for collectors.

These strengths should be protected. The most useful AI role is likely to improve preparation and consistency—not replace final sourcing, authenticity, or pricing decisions.

## Main constraints and unknowns

- Leads are reviewed manually across several sources, so promising items may be seen too late.
- Pricing evidence is stored inconsistently, making it difficult to compare estimates with actual outcomes.
- Listing creation is repetitive, but important condition details vary by item.
- The owner has not yet defined acceptable error rates for price estimates or lead alerts.
- It is not established which sources permit automated collection, monitoring, or reuse of listing data.
- Available margin may not justify paid data, automation, or advertising until a small pilot demonstrates value.

## Suggested AI opportunities

### 1. Lead triage assistant — best first candidate

Create a daily review list from owner-approved sources. The assistant could identify candidate items, normalize basic details, flag missing information, and explain why a lead may deserve attention.

**AI may:** summarize listings, identify possible matches to a saved interest list, detect obvious information gaps, and rank items for review.

**A person must:** confirm that monitoring is permitted, inspect the original listing, judge authenticity and condition, contact sellers, and approve every purchase.

**Why first:** It addresses a frequent bottleneck while keeping financial commitments under direct owner control.

### 2. Pricing evidence worksheet — promising after data cleanup

Build a comparison worksheet using verified completed sales, condition, completeness, edition, venue, fees, shipping, and date. AI could summarize comparable evidence and propose a range with a confidence note.

**AI may:** organize comparable sales, highlight inconsistent records, calculate scenario ranges, and state why confidence is limited.

**A person must:** validate the comparables, account for authenticity and condition, set the final purchase ceiling, and approve the selling price.

**Important boundary:** AI-generated prices should never be represented as appraisals or guaranteed values.

### 3. Listing and campaign drafts — useful with review

Generate first drafts for marketplace listings, customer alerts, newsletters, and social posts from an owner-approved item record.

**AI may:** produce channel-specific drafts, suggest titles and keywords, and create variations for different collector interests.

**A person must:** verify every factual claim, disclose repairs or uncertainty, confirm image rights, and approve publication.

## Proposed staged experiment

Begin with an **offline comparison** of the current review method and a simple interest-profile checklist using invented or specifically approved copied records. Record time, useful leads, errors, and missed condition or authenticity caveats. Do not connect to, monitor, or reconstruct a live source whose permission is unresolved. Consider AI ranking only if the simpler method leaves a measured gap and the owner separately approves that next test.

If the offline comparison is worthwhile and live-source permission is verified, propose a **separately approved 30-day lead-triage pilot** using one permitted source and one narrow category, such as 1980s action figures in original packaging. That later pilot would:

1. Record how the owner currently finds and evaluates leads for one week.
2. Define a simple interest profile: brands, years, condition, maximum purchase amount, excluded items, and required evidence.
3. Have AI prepare a private daily candidate list; do not allow it to contact sellers or place orders.
4. Require the owner to label each suggestion useful, irrelevant, duplicate, too late, or too risky.
5. Compare the pilot with the manual baseline before expanding.

### Measures of success

- review time saved per week
- percentage of suggestions judged relevant
- number of useful leads discovered earlier than the current process
- false-positive and duplicate rate
- estimated margin on purchased items compared with actual margin after fees and shipping
- owner confidence that important context is visible before a decision

### Stop or revise if

- the source prohibits the monitoring method
- irrelevant or duplicate suggestions create more work than they save
- important condition or authenticity warnings are repeatedly missed
- the owner begins relying on the ranking without reviewing original evidence
- purchase decisions exceed the agreed pilot limit

## Not ready for automation

Automated purchasing, seller negotiation, authenticity claims, appraisal language, final pricing, and automatic public posting are not recommended from the information assumed here. These actions carry financial, reputational, contractual, or customer-trust consequences that require better evidence and explicit controls.

## Suggested next owner decision

Decide whether saving time on lead review is valuable enough to justify a small offline comparison. A connected 30-day test would require a later decision on source permission, product category, budget, stop conditions, and who can stop it.

## Advisory acknowledgment

This fictional assessment illustrates a possible starting model, not a guaranteed business outcome. A real business should evaluate source terms, finances, customer expectations, privacy, intellectual property, and other risks carefully. AI and this assessment should not be the sole basis for consequential decisions. The free framework does not replace qualified legal, financial, appraisal, security, or business advice.
